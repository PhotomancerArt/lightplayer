# The three domes — ground truth for Yona's dome installs

Recorded from Yona, 2026-08-28. Three real structures share the word
"dome" and agents kept conflating them; this doc is the registry of
what each one actually is, so plans, examples, and conversations use
the same names. When in doubt, link here instead of re-describing a
dome from memory.

**Terminology ruling:** "dome scale" means the **big dome** — ~20-30k
LEDs, networked controllers, desktop-class firmware. It never means
the small dome, and the example projects are not "dome scale" until
one models the big dome.

| Name | Structure | LEDs | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zook dome** | non-geodesic dome by Bob Zook | ~1,500 | 2026 experiment; didn't go to that year's burn |
| **Small Dome** | 16' 2V geodesic on a riser ring | ~6.3k (50×119 + door) | built successfully since 2013, runs old LightPlayer |
| **Big dome (radiance)** | 38' 5V geodesic | ~20-30k (190×119 panels + lit vertices) | at Burning Man since 2013; the aspirational scale target |

## Zook dome

An experiment in 2026 that didn't go anywhere for that Burning Man: a
non-geodesic dome made by Bob Zook, ~1,500 LEDs — one strut path
repeated with 5-way symmetry, five controller ports of 300 LEDs each.
The as-built plugging was a permutation: sector *k* landed on port
`(1,5,4,3,2)[k]`.

Example: `examples/zook-dome` (full scale, 1,500 lamps).

## Small Dome

Yona's 16' 2-frequency geodesic dome, built successfully since 2013
and run by the old (lp2014) LightPlayer. The layout sheet ("Small
Dome, Fall Market 2023") lives at Dropbox
`Shares/LaP Projects/raDiANCE/2020 Small Dome/Overall Structure.ai`.

- **Structure**: 16' diameter, 12' tall; 2V shell hoisted on a riser
  ring. Struts: 30 short (5-bundle), 35 long (6-bundle), 30 bottom
  (5-bundle), 2 door struts (~10' each), all squished-and-bolted 1"
  EMT.
- **Panels**: **50** lit panels in 4 transport racks — the 40 2V
  faces plus one rung below the shell, which keeps only its 10
  downward-pointing triangles. Each panel is a 2'-tall triangle of
  8 mm Lucite LUX light-guide, wrapped with 5 mm WS2812B strip at
  60 LED/m — **119 LEDs per panel** — suspended by straps inside the
  strut triangle.
- **Door**: ONE chevron — a big triangle occupying the space of four
  small ones, no bottom edge — with door lights along its two struts.
- **Control**: two control boxes plus one master box. Each control
  box houses a PixLite long-range (32-output) with 2× 300 W 24 V
  PSUs and a ~50 W 12 V PSU for the board, an Ethernet switch, two
  etherCONs, a powerCON in and a pass-through out, and 32 CNLINKO
  4-pin panel connectors, 16 per side. Each box feeds half the dome;
  the door lights hang off box 1's run.

Example: `examples/small-dome` (full scale — 50×119 + 360-lamp door =
6,310 lamps; regenerated by `cargo run -p lpt-geodome`). Before
2026-08-28 this example was a 177-lamp miniature named "mini dome" —
retire that name; docs from that era (ADRs, the archetype doc's
filename) keep it only as history.

## Big dome (radiance)

Yona's 38' 5-frequency geodesic dome, taken to Burning Man every year
since 2013. **This is what "dome scale" means.**

- **Panels**: 190 edge-lit triangle panels, 119 LEDs each (~22.6k),
  plus 116 edge-lit vertices — ~20-30k LEDs total.
- **Control**: ten control boxes plus a master box, networked; the
  sector wiring diagram ("v2 Top Panel Cable Details") maps one of
  the five symmetric sectors, panels numbered per box.
- **Field truth** (lp2014 era): roughly 90% of all clicks in the old
  software were dome patching — "plug into random jacks, fix it in
  software" beats careful cabling on a build site.

No example models it yet. The scale is aspirational: desktop-class
firmware hasn't been built, but the UI/UX and engine thinking have
always aimed at this scale — it scales down well, not up.
