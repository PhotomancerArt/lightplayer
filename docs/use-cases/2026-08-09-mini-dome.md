# The mini-dome — N-way symmetric repeat, re-patched every install

The dome archetype: a structure built from **identical repeated
units** (panels, sectors, struts) whose **mapping never changes**
but whose **plugging changes every single build**. The physical
crew connects units to whatever jack is nearest and the software
is expected to sort it out afterwards.

The concrete cases:

- The zook dome: ~1500 LEDs, one strut path repeated with 5-way
  symmetry, five controller ports of 300 LEDs each. The as-built
  plugging is a permutation — sector *k* landed on port
  `(1,5,4,3,2)[k]` — which today lives hand-coded in the example's
  channel order: a patch file trying to be born.
- The radiance dome (lp2014 era): ~190 panels of 119 LEDs across
  ten controllers, plus always-lit door fixtures sharing outputs.
  Roughly **90% of all clicks** in the old software were dome
  patching, because "plug into random jacks, fix it in software"
  beats careful cabling on a build site.

## Demands on the system

Status marks as of slice 2 (2026-08-10):

- ✅ **Instance-grain patching.** The patchable unit is the repeat
  instance (a panel, a sector) — a UI grain over object-relative
  storage; storage is `(object path, optional range)` — a path into
  the mapping's object tree (`/sector/2`), so instance boundaries
  re-derive from the mapping and entries survive the structure
  growing (patch format 2, D46).
- ✅ **Stable object identity** in the mapping doc, so patch entries
  survive edits to *other* objects (map2d format 3).
- ✅ **Per-entry direction and rotation.** A panel plugged backwards
  needs `reversed`; a symmetric panel seated in the wrong
  orientation needs an offset stepped by a meaningful stride (the
  panel's edge length) — "rotation is fake but very useful." The
  stride is intrinsic where the shape has one (a closed polygon's
  lamps per side) and an authored override where it doesn't (the
  example's open chevron doors carry `"stride": 3`).
- ✅ **Multi-port, multi-output scatter.** Instances land on arbitrary
  ports of arbitrary outputs; the patch view must show every port
  as a row and every instance's destination.
- ✅ **Bulk operations.** Swap two ports, shift a port's contents,
  clear the patch for a *selection* of fixtures (the dome
  re-patches every install; the orb next to it is wired forever) —
  the patch surface's verbs (slice 2 P6).
- ⏸ **Live hardware feedback.** Select an instance, see it pulse on
  the physical build (the lp2014 `debugChannels` workflow —
  patching is done standing next to the dome). CHIPPED in slice 2
  (Q27): the surface highlights client-side; the sim/hardware pulse
  is a follow-up.

## Example project

**Shipped** with slice 2: `examples/mini-dome` — the mini RADIANCE
stand-in, deliberately **many-to-many** (Yona steer, 2026-08-09:
the peach is many-to-one, real zook is one-to-many; nobody covered
fixtures *sharing* outputs). TWO fixtures × TWO named outputs:

- `dome`: one path object `{id: "sector"}` repeated 5-way, 30 LEDs
  per sector — three suspended triangle panels of 10 lamps each,
  traced as one gapped path (the `gaps` are the panel-to-panel
  jumper wires) — patch carrying the as-built permutation with one
  sector reversed and one rotated by exactly one panel.
- `doors`: three chevron doors `{id: "door"}` (9 lamps up one leg
  and over the other, authored `"stride": 3` — the radiance
  always-lit doors), interleaved into the port TAILS on both
  outputs, sharing ports with sectors; one door rotated by a
  stride.

The map2d geometry (2026-08-12) is a plan view of the real
structure below: the five sectors' panel chains radiate from the
zenith, and the doors' feet reach the riser's ground circle.

Engine truth pinned by `lp-cli`/`lpc-engine` tests
(`mini_dome_scatter.rs`, the A1 golden oracle); the guided tour is
`docs/user-guide/patching-the-dome.md`.

## The real mini dome (as built)

Recorded from Yona, 2026-08-12. Most of this **cannot be modeled
yet** — it is kept here so future modeling work (power topology,
connector-level patching, per-panel addressing) has the ground
truth to aim at.

- **Structure**: a 2-frequency geodesic dome, 16' diameter, struts
  ~5' of 1" EMT, squished and bolted the normal way. The dome is
  hoisted on a ring of riser struts (essentially a triangle strip)
  to give it standing height.
- **Doors**: chevrons — a big triangle occupying the space of four
  small ones, with no bottom edge.
- **Glow**: the struts are bare; the light is **triangular panels
  suspended by straps inside the strut triangles**. Each panel is a
  2'-tall triangle of 8 mm Lucite LUX light-guide panel, wrapped
  with 5 mm WS2812B strip at 60 LED/m — **119 LEDs per panel**.
- **Control**: two control boxes, each housing a PixLite long-range
  (32-output) with 2× 300 W 24 V PSUs and a ~50 W 12 V PSU for the
  board, an Ethernet switch, two etherCONs, a powerCON in and a
  powerCON pass-through out, and 32 CNLINKO 4-pin panel connectors,
  16 per side. (The example's two named outputs — "1", "Box 2" —
  are these boxes in miniature.)

Provenance: mapping & patching surface vision, 2026-08-09
(planning dir `2026-08-09-0048-mapping-patching-surface`); lp2014
field experience (radiance).
