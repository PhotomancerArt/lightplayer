# ADR: The S3 brings its octal PSRAM up in the core, at a fixed window, behind `psram`

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-22
- **Deciders:** Photomancer
- **Supersedes:** None
- **Superseded by:** None
- **Plan:** `lp2025/2026-10-20-2335-s3-radio-and-ota` (M10; the evidence is
  the PSRAM spike, `psram-spike/report.md` there, branch
  `claude/spike-s3-psram-6a95bf` @ `8b0b25385`; the emulator's model is PR
  #1257)

## Context

With Bluetooth and Wi‑Fi up the S3's heap is 262,160 B, about 131 KB free at
idle with no project, against the C6's 372,344 B; a 620-pixel project left
86 KB free with Bluetooth alone. The canonical board (the plain Seeed XIAO
ESP32-S3, an ESP32-S3R8) and the desk's FS3 (the XIAO ESP32-S3 Plus) both
carry **8 MB of octal PSRAM** in the package, which nothing used.

The spike measured it on FS3 (silicon, PSRAM at 80 MHz, 620 px on four
outputs, Bluetooth advertising), against PSRAM up with the heap internal:
the whole Rust heap in PSRAM cost **3.6 % of render fps and 5 % of compile
time** and moved 154,648 B out of internal RAM (internal free while running:
86 KB → 241 KB); allocations of 4 KB and up alone cost nothing measurable and
moved 11 KB. Streaming reads run at 58 MB/s, writes at 34 MB/s, a miss costs
~440 ns; atomics work; the JIT corpus passes 13/13 from PSRAM; tree-store
erases and programs leave a dirty PSRAM heap intact.

Three things the spike found shape everything below:

- esp-hal maps PSRAM "one past the highest mapped MMU entry". On a plain image
  that is anywhere from entry `0x13` to `0x2B`; on the split image, whose
  engine, `lpfs` and scratch windows sit at `0x100..0x200`, 8 MiB does not fit
  at all.
- The RMT refill ISR can read a frame out of PSRAM, but its margin shrinks
  from 4/9 to 7/9 of the deadline when the bus is busy.
- JIT code written into PSRAM runs stale until the data cache is written back
  and the instruction cache invalidated over it.

## Decision

1. **A non-default feature, `psram`.** Turning it on in the shipped image is
   Yona's call on the walk's numbers (M10's Q-M10a). Off, every image is
   byte-for-byte what it was: every change below is behind `psram` (or the
   esp-alloc fork's `external-first`, which only `psram` enables).
2. **A fixed window, `lp_bootctl::s3_mmu::PSRAM_WINDOW` = entries
   `0x80..0x100`** (data `0x3C80_0000`, code `0x4280_0000`, 8 MiB), carved out
   of the top of the core's per-build entries, which now end at `0x80`. The
   loader's claim (a core's flash below `0x1C0`) is unchanged, so no loader
   update is involved. `lp-fw-split` refuses a core segment that reaches the
   window. The esp-hal fork's seventh diff lets the caller name the first
   entry (`PsramConfig::mmu_start_entry`).
3. **Up in the core, before anything maps `lpfs` or the engine**, so a
   core-only board has it too: right after the heap is installed. Only on a
   part whose eFuse `PSRAM_CAP` says 8 MB (ESP-IDF's S3 eFuse table, code 1 —
   the S3R8's octal part), in octal mode (`Psram::new_octal`, the fork's
   octal-only path), at the window's 8 MiB. A **boot self-test** of a few
   milliseconds — one compare-and-swap and a sampled, address-derived
   pattern, one word in every 4 KiB — must pass, or PSRAM stays off for that
   boot. Every boot says which in one `[INIT] psram:` line; a part without
   PSRAM, a driver that finds none or a failed self-test boots as an image
   without the feature.
4. **The init's IRAM comes out of the arena, not the stack.** esp-hal's
   octal init is `#[ram]`. The fork's seventh diff stops linking the
   quad-SPI path (`new_octal`) and keeps the register decode out of line in
   flash: `.rwtext` grows 2,572 B over the default image instead of 5,364 B.
   The arena gives back 3 KiB under `psram`, so the main stack is 54,880 B
   (default image: 54,392 B).
5. **The heap.** PSRAM is one esp-alloc region tagged `External` only,
   registered after every internal one. Where Rust's default allocations go
   is the build-time knob `LP_PSRAM_HEAP` until Q-M10a is ruled: `all` (the
   default: PSRAM first, every size), `split:<bytes>` (PSRAM first at or above
   it), `last` (PSRAM only once internal is full), `off` (no region). The
   esp-alloc fork's third diff (`external-first`) carries the threshold
   (`EXTERNAL_FIRST_MIN`), a sixth region, a `Reserved` capability and
   figures that exclude a capability. **The radio's region stops borrowing
   `External`**: on a `psram` image it is `Internal | Reserved`.
6. **What must not live in PSRAM, and how each is kept out:**

   | what | why | how |
   |---|---|---|
   | the LED frame the RMT refill ISR reads | the ISR's margin (spike §3) | the S3 RMT output copies a frame rendered into PSRAM into an internal buffer before `send_blocking` (31 µs for 620 × 3 B on FS3) |
   | the ISRs' code and state | an ISR must not wait on a cache miss into PSRAM | IRAM code and `static`s, as today |
   | the radio's C heap and DMA buffers | the radio's DMA reaches internal RAM only | the blobs ask for `Internal`, the radio region is `Internal \| Reserved` |
   | thread stacks | esp-rtos asks for `Internal` | unchanged |
   | anything another core touches during a flash write | core 1 hung FS3 fetching flash during an erase | core 1 stays parked (the plan's convention) |

   Everything else of the engine's — the node graph, textures, render buffers,
   the compiler's working set, JIT code, project files read into memory —
   belongs in PSRAM.
7. **Gates read internal RAM.** `[MEM]`, the heartbeat's `free`/`used` and
   the load gates' largest block are internal RAM's on a `psram` image — the
   scarce one, and comparable with every other image's — and PSRAM has a
   `[PSRAM] free= used= largest_free=` line of its own after the ledger's
   triple. No wire field changes.
8. **JIT code in PSRAM is published.** `lpvm_native::exec_addr` maps the
   cache's data window to its instruction window (`+0x0600_0000`, the same MMU
   entries), and `JitBuffer::from_code` calls a firmware-injected routine
   (`lpvm_native::code_publish`, so the crate names no ROM) for every buffer
   in that window: the firmware's writes the data cache back
   (`Cache_WriteBack_All`) and invalidates the instruction cache over the
   code (`Cache_Invalidate_Addr`). The engine sets it at its entry.
9. **Dirty lines are written back before the split image's invalidate-all.**
   `split_core::engine_window`'s `Cache_Invalidate_DCache_All` would drop
   PSRAM's dirty lines, and the engine is mapped after the core brought
   PSRAM up; a `psram` image writes the data cache back first.
10. **The MSPI clocks have one owner per phase.** esp-hal's octal init sets
    the SPI0/SPI1 core clock and the flash divider as a side effect (core
    160 MHz, flash 80 MHz at its default PSRAM speed). Until the S3's own
    80 MHz flash change (M11, PR #1260) lands, PSRAM's init is the only
    writer; when it does, the order is: M11's switch first, then PSRAM's
    init, which must leave the flash clock M11 chose — checked at that merge,
    not assumed here.

## Consequences

- A board with PSRAM gains 8 MiB of heap for 3 KiB of internal arena; a board
  without it loses those 3 KiB and nothing else.
- The emulator models the part (`lp-emu-esp32s3`, PR #1257): the `psram`
  image boots both ways, renders the shipped image's frame byte for byte with
  and without PSRAM (`lp-cli/tests/emu_s3_link_gates.rs`), and runs the S3
  walk (`LP_WALK_S3_FEATURES=psram just walk-esp32s3-emu`). Its timing is
  modeled and ungraded: no emulated number says anything about PSRAM's speed.
- What the emulator cannot show: a stale instruction cache (its windows are
  never stale, so a missing publish passes there), dirty lines an
  invalidate-all would drop, marginal 80 MHz timing, atomics on another die.
  Those are silicon's.
- The walk (M10 P6) is owed: FS3 left the bus after the spike's last flash and
  needs hands. Its rows — 620 px with Bluetooth connected across the policy
  sweep, Wi‑Fi joined, a tree-store save while rendering, 20 warm resets, first
  light against M11's image — go in
  `docs/reports/2026-10-XX-s3-psram-walk.md`, and Q-M10a is put to Yona with
  them.
