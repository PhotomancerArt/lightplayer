Clock transport hero — the widget IS the debug drawer

Thesis (G3 gate, PR #335): the clock card's OUTPUT hero stops being a churning digits counter and becomes a time instrument — it shows the passage of time and it is the transport: run/pause, speed, and scrubbing live in the widget, and the Debug drawer's Running / Rate / Scrub offset rows go away. Every concept below drives the same three slots — one shared clock — so touching any instrument moves all of them, including the phasor traces (scrub-exact, like the engine). Digits are calm (whole seconds); sub-second motion is analog.

2 · Sweepwatch — stopwatch dial · drag the face to scrub · speed knob

A stopwatch: the sweep hand (1 rev = 60 s) carries all the sub-second motion, so the digits can sit still at whole seconds. The minutes sub-dial gives long runtimes a face. Drag anywhere on the dial to scrub (clockwise = forward, 1 turn = 1 min); the bezel goes amber while off-live. Speed is the log knob (׼–×8, detent at ×1, double-click resets).

3 · Platter — jog wheel · spin it to scrub · DJ pitch fader for speed

A turntable: the platter itself rotates with clock time (1 rev = 10 s) — the rim dot makes speed visible without any number. Grab and spin the platter to jog: fine, tactile scrubbing (1 turn = 10 s). Center button = run/pause. The vertical fader is speed — center line = ×1, up = fast, snaps at the detent.

4 · Tape — scrolling timeline under a fixed playhead · drag the tape · rate chips

Time as tape: ticks stream right-to-left under a fixed playhead — passage of time is literal motion, and speed changes read as tape speed. Drag the tape to scrub (direct manipulation: the moment under the playhead is the clock time); the clock's birth (0:00) is a hard edge. Speed is a chip row — discrete detents rather than a knob.

5 · Hairline — one-row transport strip · the smallest thing that could work

The minimal counter-argument: a single 28 px row. Sweep ring (1 rev = 10 s) shows passage; digits stay whole-second; drag the digits horizontally to scrub; the ×1 chip drags like a value slider (double-click resets). If the hero shouldn't compete with the phasors for attention, this is the floor.
