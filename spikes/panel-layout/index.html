Panel layout — one rule, every surface

A panel's controls come in shapes, not widgets: a cell (a knob, the Speed knob, a toggle), a strip (a fader, a palette) and a block (the Pattern instrument). Today each surface lays the same panel out its own way, in an order nobody chose: the walk's order, which is the files' and slots' names sorted (core keeps them in a sorted VecMap). That is why the choker's board shows Brightness, Speed, Palette, Scale, and its Scale knob sits alone under the Palette. Thesis: one layout rule, by shape, on every surface: the master fader first, then each group's cells, then its strips, every group under the same quiet heading. The surface's width only changes how many cells fit on a line, and, on wide surfaces, whether groups sit side by side.

Real panels: PLAYFUL choker (catalog/projects/playful-choker, in the board's real order), Aurora Sign · 15 (card_many_controls_panel, four effect groups), PLAYFUL tryout (a Pattern-picker piece, pattern_picker_fixtures), a made-up Porch lamp with two strips between its knobs (the stress case), and a one-knob panel with no master. Everything is live: drag knobs up and down, faders sideways (touching a control holds it, gold, as in Studio; ↺ lets go), press the Pattern instrument. A control is one control: the same knob moves in every column.

1The shapes

Every control on a panel is one of three shapes, and a layout only needs to know the shape. Footprints are dashed. The faded ones aren't built; they are here to test whether the three shapes hold up as more widgets arrive. They do: each of them is a cell, a strip or a block.

2Order inside a group: the card at 300 px

The same panel and the same card under four rules. The master follows the Master switch (a fader by default, as Yona asked). Panel order is what the board does now. Try PLAYFUL choker (Scale alone under the Palette) and Porch lamp (two strips between knobs, the worst case for panel order).

Panel order: a cell after a strip starts a new line, so a knob can sit alone; with two strips the knobs break into three runs.

Cells → strips: each group's knobs and toggles are one run, then its wide things close the group. No cell is ever alone unless the group has one. It reads like a mixer: the knobs above, the faders under them.

Strips → cells: the master and the palette stack into a list at the top, and the knobs sit below. It's tidy, but the strips run together into a column of bars and the knobs lose their place near the top.

Dense (grid-auto-flow: dense): panel order, with later cells pulled up into holes. It equals cells → strips until more cells follow than the hole holds. Then it splits them, and nobody can predict where a knob lands.

8Authored order

Should an author be able to set the order, and what is the default when they don't? With the shape rule, the default only has to order the controls within a run of cells or strips. That stays the walk's order (sorted names), with the clock's Speed first after the master, as it is the panel's primary speed control (clock-transport ADR §7). An authored order is a change to the project format, so it's Yona's call. Middle: what arranging could look like in the editor.

9Leans and questions

Lean: C, which is A on the card and on a phone. One rule by shape on every surface: the master fader first, each group's cells then its strips, and every group under the card's quiet heading (no boxes). Groups become tiles side by side only where a surface holds two or more tiles (the editor's card, a desktop). The card part is small and could land in #1196 now.
  1. Three shapes. Is every panel control a cell (knob, Speed, toggle), a strip (fader, palette) or a block (the Pattern instrument), with every surface laying out by shape and never by widget? Lean: yes. Core names the shape beside the widget, so the card, the play page and the editor can't drift apart again.
  2. The master is a fader, first, on a line of its own, on every surface. That undoes #1196's brightness knob on panels, so the fixture card and the panel use one widget again (P16). Lean: yes. D (in the frame) is tempting on a phone, where the bar sits in thumb reach, but a fader laid over the card's live picture competes with the main surface (P18). Not now.
  3. Inside a group: cells, then strips, then blocks (§2, second card). Lean: yes. No knob is left alone under a palette, and a group's wide things close it, like faders under a mixer's knobs. Strips → cells stacks the bars into a list at the top. Dense moves knobs unpredictably.
  4. Nested groups: one treatment everywhere, the card's quiet heading on a hairline. The play page and the editor drop their bordered boxes, and wide surfaces set groups side by side as tiles (C). Lean: yes.
  5. Shared lines (B): drop it? It saves a line on the card, but a palette would then be 132 px on one card and 284 px on the next, and lines of knobs and bars go ragged (P3, P8). Lean: drop it.
  6. Authored order: not yet? The shape rule fixes what looked wrong. Inside a run, the order stays the walk's (sorted names), with the clock's Speed leading. If authors ask later, it's an ordered panel list on the module (§8), which changes the project format. Lean: not yet.
  7. Order of work: land the card part in #1196 (a master fader, cells then strips), then plan the play page, the editor and C's tiles from this spike. Lean: yes.
