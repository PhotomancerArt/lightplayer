Panel layout — round 2

Round 1 settled two things. Every control is one of three shapes: a cell (knob, Speed, toggle), a strip (fader, palette) or a block (the Pattern instrument). And brightness is a fader, first, but not as a special case (Yona: "the bus should handle this?"). This round asks how the bus puts brightness first (§2), how a complex project separates its global controls from the ones that belong to one zone or one pattern (§3), and tries Yona's three changes to the look: a strip's label above its track, light lines between controls, and the whole cell as the hit area, with a hover.

Everything is live. Drag anywhere in a cell (up and down for a knob, sideways for a strip); touching a control holds it, gold, as in Studio, and ↺ lets go. Press a toggle's cell, or the Pattern instrument. A control is one control: the same knob moves in every column. ?data=smalldome&lines=off opens the page in a state.

1The shapes (settled)

Drawn in round 2's look. A strip carries its label and value above the track, as the palette always did, so the track is the whole width whatever the label says. Light lines divide the controls, and every cell is its own hit area: hover one.

2Brightness first, from the bus

The bus already says what brightness is. Its registry of well-known channels (lpc-model/src/bus/well_known.rs) lists brightness as "Master output brightness (0-1); fixtures consume it by default", and the fixture's slot is default-bound to it with panel = "show". The special case is only in the panel code: the card's MASTER_CHANNEL hoist, and #1196's knob on panels. Drop both, and rank by the registry instead.

// A group's order, with nothing named in the panel code. // 1. its strips, then its blocks, then its cells (the shape) // 2. inside each run, the bus registry's order (well_known.rs) // brightness · palette · clock.rate · …, then everything else // 3. then the walk's order (as today) pub const MASTER_CHANNEL: &str = "brightness"; // board_panel_picks.rs fixture.panel_brightness() // #1196's knob on panels rank(channel) = WELL_KNOWN_CHANNELS.position(channel)
Brightness is a strip, and the registry ranks it before the palette, so it leads every group that has one: the root's, or a zone's own. The clock's Speed leads the cells the same way. Nothing in the card, the play page or the editor knows the word "brightness". And the fixture card and the panel draw one widget again, a fader.

Try small-dome (a real catalog project). Its dome and its doors each have their own fixture, so each has its own brightness, and nothing at the root has one. Today's card hoists the dome's brightness to the front with no heading, so it reads as the whole project's. Ranked by the bus, each zone's brightness leads its own zone.

Today: panel order (the walk's: files and slots in sorted order), with the card pulling the first brightness it finds to the front, even a nested one.

Strips → cells, by the bus: no exception anywhere. The strips stack at the top in the registry's order (brightness, then palette), then the knobs. With labels above and lines between, the stack reads as a set of bars rather than a list.

Cells → strips, by the bus: the knobs come first, so brightness lands under them unless the registry also marks it as a "lead". That's one more fact in the bus, but it's still an exception.

Dense: panel order with later cells pulled into holes. Where a knob lands still can't be predicted.

3Global and specific: complex projects

The bus already draws the line, as scopes. The root scope is global: what stays put across everything (the multi-pattern vision's shared knobs: Speed, Brightness, Palette, Detail). A nested module is a zone (small-dome's dome and doors, Aurora's effects). The playing pattern is its own scope, and its knobs come and go with it (at most two). A zone's control with no writer of its own follows the global one (violet: Aurora's plasma speed), until someone touches it. What's open is how to show that. Four ways, on three real projects.

Project
G1Headings. Round 1's A: the global controls have no heading, and each zone sits under its name. "Global" is only implied, by being first and unnamed. G2Global band. The global controls sit on a band of their own, named for what they reach ("Everything", or "All patterns" on a playlist). On a phone the band stays pinned while the zones scroll under it, and on a desktop it's a column beside them. Zone headings say what they are: "Scanner · this pattern". G3One zone at a time. The global controls, then a switch between the zones, and only one zone's controls. On a playlist the Pattern instrument is already that switch. It's compact, but it hides controls: "a panel you have to unfold before you can play it is not a control panel". G4Channel strips. A mixer: one column per zone, the global column first, knobs above and faders at the foot (vertical). Each zone's brightness stands at the foot of its own column.

10Leans and questions

Lean: one rule from the bus, with the global controls on a band. A group is its strips, then its blocks, then its cells, ranked by the bus's registry. The root's controls sit on a band named for their reach, pinned on a phone and a column on a desktop (G2). Zones go under headings that say what they are, as tiles where the surface is wide (C). Every control is drawn with its label above, light lines between, and the whole cell to hit.
  1. Brightness first, from the bus: strips before cells, ranked by the registry's order, and nothing named "brightness" in panel code. This deletes MASTER_CHANNEL and #1196's panel knob, so the fixture card and the panel use one fader again. Lean: yes. It also fixes small-dome, where today's card puts the dome's brightness first as if it were the project's.
  2. Global and specific: G2, the global band, named for its reach, pinned on a phone and a column on a desktop, with zones under headings that say what they are ("Dome", "Scanner · this pattern"). Lean: G2. G1 doesn't say which brightness is which. G3 hides zones and their held gold. G4 is lovely on a desktop and breaks on a card.
  3. Light lines between controls: edge to edge (P10), a step lighter than a group's own line. Lean: yes. They make the panel read as a panel, and the strips stop looking like a list.
  4. The whole cell is the hit area, with a hover: drag anywhere in a knob's cell, press anywhere in a toggle's. A strip drags from anywhere too; pressing its track still jumps. In Studio today the label opens the control's details, so: Lean: a press without a drag opens the details, and a toggle's details go to its ⓘ. Your call.
  5. Shared lines (B): drop it? With labels above, a shared line puts a strip's header beside a knob's dial at a different height, and a palette's width changes from card to card. Lean: drop it.
  6. Authored order: still not yet? Lean: not yet (§9).
  7. Order of work: #1196 takes the bus rank and the fader (small, core-only). The look (labels above, lines, the whole cell) and the band go in one plan across the card, the play page and the editor. Lean: yes.

Pass looks like a yes, no or change on A–G, plus a G-letter if it isn't G2.
