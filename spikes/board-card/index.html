Boards — the vocabulary, and the card built from it

Round 2. Round 1 settled the direction of the card (picture first) and showed the real problem: a newcomer can’t tell what each word on it means. So this round defines the vocabulary first and builds the card from it. The spine is the stack: every board is project (what it plays), firmware (what it runs) and board (the hardware, where it is, and how we reach it). Every fact, notice and action belongs to one layer. The order on a card (Yona): what it plays / what it is → where it is and how we’re connected → notices, only when something’s wrong. Everything else is on the board’s page, or shown when it becomes relevant. The page so far uses the lean for every question below; the questions are lettered to match the chat. Click a card to see what Open does; hover for its ⋯ menu.

2The card, part by part

The cold-read test: every element should say what it is without knowing the model. Each number is the sentence a newcomer should be able to say.

  1. The picture is the board’s lights. Moving means you’re seeing it now. Still and dim means this is how it looked when it was last seen.
  2. The status is always there. When the board is fine, its frame rate (“60 fps”, blue for live). When something needs you, a notice takes its place, with the one action that fixes it. (Q4)
  3. The name is what it is. One name. A board you named (Porch) shows what it plays on the next line; a board you didn’t is just its project’s name. (Q3)
  4. How we reach it. A cloud: reachable from anywhere. A plug, Bluetooth or Wi‑Fi mark: connected that way, right now. (Q5)
  5. What it plays, when that’s not its name, and how many boards play it in sync. (Q6)
  6. Where it is: a folder for your groups, a face for someone else’s board.
  7. Everything else is one hover away (⋯), grouped by layer, or on the board’s page. The whole card opens the board in Studio.

8Prior art (reference)

From a survey of Sonos, Apple Home, Hue, WLED, Govee/Nanoleaf/Twinkly, Spotify Connect, take-over patterns (WordPress, Office, Meet, Teams, WhatsApp), Google Drive and Figma, and fleet tools (ESPHome, Home Assistant, balena, Particle). What recurs:

  1. Name things by place or purpose; a default name is a placeholder (Sonos rooms; WLED shows the address until you name it; “(New Device)”).
  2. When healthy, show the content, not a status word (Sonos shows what’s playing; Apple “3 on”; WLED tints the card with the live colour).
  3. But a picture alone can’t prove life: an effect that’s dark looks dead. WLED changes the card when offline; ESPHome still prints ONLINE.
  4. Offline is a time, not a yes/no: “Offline, 5 minutes ago” (WLED), “Last seen” (Home Assistant).
  5. Wait before saying offline (WLED waits 60 s; Google Home’s brief false “offline” is a well-known annoyance).
  6. Push unhealthy things down (WLED’s “Offline Devices” section). Hiding them reads as “deleted” (Sonos).
  7. Synced devices: one name plus a count (Sonos “Kitchen + 2”), or a named group (Twinkly, Govee).
  8. In use elsewhere: say who, then a plain verb (“Take over” in WordPress, “Switch here” in Meet, “Use here” in WhatsApp), offer a gentler choice, and tell the person who lost it.
  9. Shared things show a person (Drive: owner and date; people search by owner).
  10. Reach is said in words, not transport icons (Apple: “Control Accessories Remotely”). No surveyed product puts USB/Bluetooth/cloud icons on the main tile.
  11. Updates: a small badge on the tile, the action in settings, progress in words (“Update Available”, “Installing (42%)”). Say “Updating” only for firmware.
  12. A new device is its own state with an adopt verb (ESPHome “DISCOVERED · Take Control”; Home Assistant “set it up”). Status is never colour alone (shapes in WLED, bar plus word in ESPHome).

Sources include support.sonos.com, support.apple.com (102056, 102386), the WLED-Native-Android strings and theme, github.com/esphome/dashboard, the Home Assistant frontend translations, docs.balena.io device statuses, docs.particle.io, WordPress wp-admin/includes/post.php, support.google.com/meet/answer/14762432, support.google.com/drive/answer/2375057. Govee, Nanoleaf and Hue publish little official wording; their cells were thin.
