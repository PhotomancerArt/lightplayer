The card’s rows are the setup list

Sean flashed his first board on 2026-10-18 and nothing asked for his Wi‑Fi. Round 1 compared four ways to ask; Yona chose none of them and set a direction: the rows of the card are actually a great benefit here. its almost a todo list. all we have to do is call out each item. Round 2 drew that as eight rows. Round 3 answers his notes on it.

Decided so far (Yona, 2026-10-19)

  1. Three principles. Make it obvious what to do. Teach the UI and keep things in place, so setup isn’t a one-off. Minimize decisions, especially repeat decisions.
  2. The rows are the setup list, eight of them: Project, USB, Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, lightplayer.app, Access, Firmware, Hardware. lightplayer.app has its own row for privacy (“lightplayer.app - disabled” is a nice clean thing to say) and for its journey: the cloud icon, the name, an action.
  3. Save to the account, with permission; a remembered network is said on the card.
  4. Round 2: this is good. Its leans stand on this page (“disabled” for a radio turned off, the account mark, lightplayer.app naming the account, the second board joining with no press) unless you say otherwise.

1What wants attention

The same moment, five ways: installed, signed out, no project. Three rows have something to do (Project Add, Wi‑Fi Set up, lightplayer.app Sign in). The mark goes only on a row that has something left to do, never on a problem (those keep their orange or red) and never on a row someone disabled. Today’s card is first, for where we started.

2Walk it

  • Install firmware, then watch which rows light up. The primary moves straight to Add a project.
  • Set up on the Wi‑Fi row opens its details: the networks it hears, and Disable Wi‑Fi under them. Either one ends the row’s mark.
  • Sign in on lightplayer.app, or open its row and Disable lightplayer.app.
  • Press the name, type, Enter.
  • Press any row to open its details; press it again to close them. Bluetooth’s switch works too.
  • The strip’s signed in · second board joins your network with no press, and the Wi‑Fi row never asks.

3Every state, across a board’s life

Frozen, so the rows and the primaries scan as a set. The strip’s Attention applies here too; the account and the join don’t.

4The name

Today the name is renamed from Hardware’s details, and nothing on the name says so. The name is the thing you look at, so it is edited there: press it, type, Enter or click away saves, Esc leaves it as it was. Said once (P2): the Name field leaves Hardware’s details.

5The rows’ words

Each row in the card’s grammar, icon · kind · name · state · action. The four link rows have no kind word: the link’s name is its kind. The state says the fact and the button says the fix, so the two never repeat each other.

RowSaysWhen

6Your account

Asked once, then never again. The first time a signed-in person adds a network, Remember in your account is on: that switch is the permission. Every board after that joins your remembered networks with no press, and a small account mark at the row’s end says where it came from. The account’s settings are a card of rows in the same grammar: what it remembers (networks, passwords), and what a new board starts with (lightplayer.app, Bluetooth). Each row’s details are the one place that thing is changed. Two doors: Account settings in the avatar menu, and the account mark on any card row, which opens the account at that row.

Where a Wi‑Fi password lives is a security decision for an ADR in the plan. The precedent is the account’s play and edit passwords, which the service can already read.
