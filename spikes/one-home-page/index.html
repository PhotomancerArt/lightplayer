One home page — boards and projects together

Thesis: lightplayer.app opens on your stuff. The landing hero, /devices and /projects become one page. Order: connected and running pairs, then recent pairs, then projects not on a board, then patterns, then the catalog, unchanged. Filter: All groups by default, one control to narrow it. Each item shows its group. Connect: three buttons, USB, Bluetooth and Network. Design language: the board is the unit, with its project as what's on it. The three concepts share every piece of data and differ in structure: cards, a list, or a stage. Click any board or project to see the URL it would open. Vision: lp2025/2026-10-06-1530-boards-and-projects-model/vision.md.

The step control draws what each step of the work delivers. Step 1 uses only what this browser knows: no cloud and no groups. Step 2 adds boards in your account, their status without connecting, and groups. Step 3 adds sharing: Sean's group, access requests, and Busy.

1Today: the landing (reference)

Production as of 79668c96c. A logo hero with "Edit the logo", the agent box with its filler line, four doors, then the catalog. Boards are on Devices and your projects on Projects, one click away each. Yona: "I don't actually use it for anything."

2A · Pair cards — "the board is the card"

One grid sorted by importance, with no section headings until Patterns and Examples. Each card is a board: its LEDs on top, its name, then what it plays. A project with no board uses the same card with a poster and no board name. Filter: a chip row. Connect: three small buttons on the right of that row. A project on three boards: three cards, each marked ⛓ 3.

For: it's the most device-centric: you see your boards, and every card is the same shape and click. The live board's LEDs move, so "connected and running" is obvious without words. Against: the chip row runs out of room with many groups (try "Many groups"). An attached set repeats the project's name once per board. Projects and patterns blur together if both are cards.

3B · One list — "a pair is a line"

Dense rows: preview, board, what it plays, group, status, Open. Filter: the page title is the filter ("All groups ▾"), and picking a group puts you at that group's address (Q16). A project on three boards: one project row with its boards under it. Patterns are a strip of small chips, not rows.

For: it scales to many boards and groups, and status, group and age line up in columns you can scan. The title-as-filter costs no extra row, and its URL gives you a shareable group page. Against: it's the least visual. A home page for lights that reads like a file manager loses the "see it running" moment, and the previews are tiny.

4C · Stage — "your board is the hero"

The hero slot stays, but it shows your connected board, big and live, with one Open button. Other boards that are on now sit beside it. With nothing connected, and for a newcomer, the hero is the brand mark with the three connect buttons under it, so the page keeps its face. Filter: tabs, like the top bar. A project on three boards: one stacked tile with a status pill for each board.

For: it's the strongest north-star answer. You open lightplayer.app and the first thing you see is your lights, playing, with one button to edit them. Tiles share the catalog's look, so the whole page reads as one gallery. Against: the hero only works when a board is connected in this tab. Boards that are online elsewhere can't play there, because the page never connects just to show status, so with no connected board the hero is static. Tabs overflow like chips.

5Shared pieces: status words

Words a pair can show without connecting to the board (T7: a C6 holds one network session, so the page never dials a board for its status). The step tag says when each becomes possible. Only a board connected in this tab plays live; every other preview is a still poster of its project.

My lean: C's stage on top of A's cards.
  1. The hero is C's: your connected board, big and live, with Open. With no board connected, it's the brand mark with the three connect buttons, which is also the welcome state. That keeps the page's face, and Sean's first visit is one click from USB.
  2. Below it, A's grid in importance order, but with C's stacked tile for an attached set. Three cards that all say "Holiday Eaves" read as a mistake; one stack with three status pills reads as one project on three boards.
  3. Filter: B's title-as-filter ("All groups ▾" as a link to /g/…), not chips or tabs. It's the only one that survives "Many groups" and phone width, and it adds no row.
  4. Patterns: B's chip strip, so patterns can't be mistaken for projects (D20).
