Canvas design language — objects as THINGS

Pass-2 gate seed: objects on the mapping canvas "should feel like individual THINGS, not collections of hard-to-target tiny things." Pass 2 shipped padded convex hulls with whole-hull hit targets; this spike explores the next step on three axes: (1) the outline itself — convex hull vs a morphological offset-and-union (buffer every lamp by r, union the discs) that respects concavity; (2) the lamps — round 3: stroke + voronoi, the solid cell ribbon placed on / inside / outside the stroke to capture direction (an object provides an outline stroke and its lamp polygons); (3) overlapping-object selection — shipped v1 vs KiCad-style menu vs click-to-cycle vs prefer-same-fixture. Section 4 composes the pieces into three complete design languages on the mini-dome. Geometry is real-ish: the mini-dome's 5 sectors + 3 doors, a C-arc, the peach.

1Object outlines — convex hull vs offset-and-union

Same lamps, three treatments per row: the shipped padded convex hull, the offset-union (distance field, iso-contour at r, marching squares + Chaikin), and both overlaid — the hatched wedge is the area the convex hull claims but the object doesn't occupy: dead click-target, and visually a lie about the shape. Round 2 (gate ruling): union wins; the buffer now offsets from the polyline through lamp centres instead of per-lamp discs, which kills the scalloped edge (flip buffer: discs above to see round 1's bumps; jumps longer than ~2.6× the local spacing stay point-buffered so gaps never bridge). Drag outline r above; note the dome sectors are hollow strands, so the union honestly grows a hole (toggle fill holes to decide whether solidity should win). The thin spike at the dome apex is real shipped behaviour: the convex pad's miter clamp (4×r) flings needle-sharp corners — the union has no corner cases to clamp.

Mini-dome, 5 sectors + 3 doors. Sectors are near-convex, so the two agree on silhouette — but the union hugs the strand and (holes unfilled) shows the hollow center honestly. Doors sit over sector bases: bodies overlap, which is section 3's problem.

C-arc and peach — the concave cases. The convex hull bridges the C's mouth and the peach's cleft: clicking in the mouth of the C selects the arc even though there is nothing there. The offset-union outline is the shape a person would draw.

2Lamps — stroke + voronoi, direction by alignment

Round 3 (gate rulings): the voronoi look is the one — beams rejected ("they aren't solid") — but stroke direction must be captured, Illustrator-style: the solid voronoi cell ribbon is aligned to the path like a stroke — on path (faces the viewer), inside (a strip wrapping a form), outside (edge-lit). Cells are the round-1 derivation (Voronoi ∩ disc); each lamp shifts by its own cell radius so the band's edge kisses the path, exactly like an aligned stroke; the pale core marks the true emitter. Scope ruled IN: an object formally provides (1) an outline stroke and (2) a series of lamp polygons (as lp2014 did) — derived today, customizable someday; this section is the look-spike for that model.

Reading it: all three alignments keep the solid tiled body that made round 1 work; inside/outside add the physical fact — the C-arc aligned inside reads as a strip wrapped around a column, outside as an edge-lit rim, and the alignment is exactly the per-object authored bit ("wraps a form" / "edge-lit" / "faces viewer"). At 45% the ribbon stays a solid body where circles dissolve. Beam wedges kept only as the rejected strip.

3Overlapping objects — who gets the click?

Interleaved combs (one fixture, two objects — convex hulls would coincide almost entirely) and a door lying over a sector's base (two fixtures). Bodies are offset-union. Click the canvas — or use the sim buttons (headless capture uses them too). Shipped v1: whole hull is the target, a hull that also owns the nearest lamp wins, else last-painted. Round 2 (gate note: cycle is intuitive): the new cycle + hold/rclick menu policy keeps plain clicks on the cycle fast path and summons the candidate menu on press-and-hold (~420ms) or right-click. Round 3: ambiguity is generous — anything within ~10px of a body counts as a candidate (marked ~ in the log), so clicking near-but-not-on the overlap still cycles instead of feeling broken.

4Three complete languages — the mini-dome, hovered + selected

Everything composed: same scene, sector 1 hovered, sector 3 selected, door 1 overlapping sector 3's base. A is a faithful reproduction of what shipped in pass 2. B renders objects as tinted entities: smooth line-buffer body, per-object hue at rest, round 3: fill = the voronoi ribbon offset inward (§2's ruled look), name chip on the body; the fixture frame demotes to a corner tag. C pushes "solid" to the limit: opaque bodies with a drop shadow, lamps as lit cores. Gate ruling: B reads cleanest — C kept only for contrast.

What to judge (round 3): B with the inside-aligned ribbon — do the cells still read once live lamp colors paint them (each cell would carry its lamp's live color, the pale core stays the emitter truth)? How much per-object hue survives into production — full hue at rest, or neutral at rest with hue only on hover/selection? And do the on-body name chips scale to ~150 dome objects, or do they need a zoom threshold?
