Passwords and keys: what Studio keeps, where, and how you delete it

Sean flashed his first board and nothing remembered his Wi‑Fi. Yona wants the next board to be easy, with no surprises: If I consent (default OFF), lightplayer can store my secrets with minimal friction … I can at any time see what lightplayer stores, delete it, clear it … I can opt for browser-local storage … I can easily opt out. The thesis: one page lists every secret Studio holds, grouped by where it lives, this browser or your account. One choice says where Studio remembers the Wi‑Fi and board passwords you type: This browser (the default, signed in or out), Your account (off until you turn it on, encrypted), or Nowhere. Changing it says what happens to what is already there. The Wi‑Fi form names the place before you press Connect, and every surface that remembers links to one plain page, opened in a new tab, that says exactly how it works.

What Studio holds today (checked 2026-10-19)

  • This browser: its own device key lp.access.browser.v1 (minted at first start, login-equivalent, never synced); passwords friends shared lp.ble.passwords.v1 (typed at a board, or saved by an /unlock link without asking); a copy of the account's keys lp.access.account.v1 (so a phone offline still unlocks; removed at sign-out); the app chat's AI keys (Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, custom) inside lp.settings.v1, which the brief did not list.
  • The account (cloud): account_access (0005) holds the account key's 32-byte secret and the play/author passwords, all readable at rest; minted on the account's first ask, with no question. Backups: litestream to Tigris, 720 h retention (infra/litestream.yml).
  • Wi‑Fi passwords: never kept (network_controller.rs:33-35); the ADR calls account storage later research (2026-10-04-device-wifi-settings.md:250-254). This work supersedes that.
  • Where it shows: the home page's fold Unlocking your boards (web-built). The avatar menu is Profile · Switch account · Add another account… · Sign out; signed out, the chrome's slot is the one word Sign in, with no menu. Studio has a docs section (/docs/<page>, markdown in docs/user-guide/): the natural home for the explainer. The repo is public.

1The page

Three structures for one page, and today's fold. Each is live: open a row, Show a value, Forget one (two presses: it is Lasting), Clear a place, or change where Studio remembers. Changing the place opens a short sheet that says what happens to what is there; with nothing there it just switches. Section 2's forms add to these pages when you press Connect.

2The moment it asks

The board card's Wi‑Fi row, open on its form (the card plan's eight rows, round 3). Default off means the account is never chosen for you; minimal friction means a person who wants it gets there in one pick, without leaving the form; no surprises means the place is named before the password leaves the form. Type nothing: press Connect, then look at section 1.

The consent itself is one block, used wherever the account is turned on (the form, the page): what will be kept, how, what it gets you, a link that opens the explainer, and what happens to passwords already in this browser.

3How it works (opens in a new tab)

Every How it works ↗ opens this. It has one job: say exactly what is kept, where, who can read it, and how to delete it, in words a person reads to the end. It is honest about the one thing a person might not assume: the account's copy is encrypted at rest, not end to end, so lightplayer.app's server can read it when your browser asks. Three takes: the article (with the label first), the label alone, and the live record.
